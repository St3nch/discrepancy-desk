## Executive judgment preserved

Fable's central judgment was that the Desk's Record spine was coherent and unusually strict,
but the Product had not yet proved real operator use. It characterized the implementation as a
governed append-only evidence ledger with a content-addressed Vault, capability-separated
human Decision path, living report, and fail-closed evidence walkback.

Its strongest Product recommendation was:

> commission FILE-01 for real with the CLI as it stands and let measured friction from real
> investigation drive the next Product slice.

The report warned against building a generic investigative engine, search platform, graph,
X/social subsystem, broad UI, or autonomous research layer before the real File creates
pressure.

## FILE-01 lessons preserved

The report considered these foundations durable unless later real Files falsify them:

- Capture and Artifact remain distinct.
- Artifact and frozen Surface remain distinct.
- Locator remains an exact address into one exact preserved/frozen version.
- Excerpt remains mechanically re-derivable evidence selection.
- Observation, Claim, and human Decision remain separate.
- Discrepancy remains an investigative handle rather than a truth-bearing score.
- File remains scope of attention rather than scope of truth.
- PostgreSQL enforces append-only and human-Decision capability boundaries.
- Vault payloads remain evidence authority relative to derived Surfaces.
- evidence walkback should recompute rather than trust stored convenience copies.

The report also identified unproven pressures to measure during real commissioning:

- operator throughput and UUID/CLI friction;
- usefulness of the living report;
- full-media or full-document Surface reuse with many sub-locators;
- model participation and proposal provenance;
- acquisition routes that are not HTTP(S);
- whether existing Claim posture vocabulary is sufficient.

These are research/product pressures, not pre-approved architecture.

## External ecosystem findings preserved

The survey compared journalism, archival, provenance, annotation, citation, LLM-research,
retrieval, media-processing, social-acquisition, notebook, graph, and case-management tools.

The recurring conclusion was that external systems contain useful patterns, but none should
replace Desk authority boundaries merely because they are mature or popular.

Patterns considered useful to study when a real need appears included:

- WARC/WACZ request-response capture and manifest fixity;
- Browsertrix / ArchiveWeb.page operator-side web capture;
- Tropy image/selection/transcription separation;
- Docling provenance span shapes;
- Hypothesis redundant selectors as re-anchoring hints, never Locator rewrites;
- PREMIS / PROV terminology as possible export vocabulary;
- BagIt as a possible future File export shape;
- local OCR/transcription tools as derived-Surface producers;
- passim-style text reuse with character offsets for later source-genealogy work;
- rebuildable PostgreSQL retrieval indexes keyed back to Locator coordinates.

The report rejected turning graph edges, model confidence, risk scores, suspicion values, or
provider-native relevance scores into governed truth.

## Anthropic Citations finding

The report identified Anthropic Citations as an unusually compatible optional provider
capability because plain-text citations can return machine-readable character locations into
content supplied by the caller.

Material external source:
https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/citations

The Product-level direction preserved from that research is provider-independent:

1. the Desk controls a frozen Surface;
2. a model may propose a relevant passage and citation coordinates;
3. the Desk re-slices the frozen Surface itself;
4. any cited text/coordinates are mechanically checked;
5. the result remains Workspace/proposal material until governed admission;
6. model citation validity does not prove evidentiary support, interpretation, or truth.

Anthropic Citations is therefore an optional future experiment, not Desk architecture. A
different provider or local model should be able to participate through the same
provider-independent verification boundary.

This research is the principal external basis for current deferred item F15.

## Model and agent role preserved

The report saw the highest-value model roles as:

- reading large frozen Surfaces;
- noticing candidate contradictions, omissions, chronology tensions, quote inheritance, and
  source-local statements;
- drafting candidate questions and Claims;
- editorial drafting from governed Record;
- skeptical review.

It recommended deterministic machinery for digest verification, Locator bounds, exact text
slicing, lineage checks, and other behavior that must be reproducible.

It kept every human Decision, source-independence judgment, and publication approval under
explicit human authority.

The report proposed a closed named-operation interface rather than arbitrary SQL/model
database access. That implementation shape was not accepted as architecture; the underlying
authority rule already exists in current Desk authority.

## X and provider-restricted retention finding

The report found a material future conflict between immutable evidence preservation and some
provider terms that require stored content to be deleted, modified, synchronized, or withheld
after source-side changes.

For X specifically, the report cited then-current official developer policy/agreement terms
and concluded that API-acquired content may carry deletion/modification obligations that
cannot safely be assumed compatible with indefinite immutable Vault retention.

Current official terms should always be re-read at the time of any future provider decision;
pricing and policy are moving provider facts, not architecture.

The important provider-neutral Product question is:

> How should the Desk represent historically important material when the lawful/contractual
> acquisition route does not permit the ordinary immutable-payload lifecycle?

The report suggested an append-only retention-status/tombstone-style approach, but Steward
reconciliation did **not** accept that as a solution. Merely marking retained content as
deleted may not satisfy provider terms that require deletion or modification of stored
content.

A separate discussion with CHAZ also distinguished:

- preserving or quoting what a publication itself reported;
- embedding a live provider object that may later disappear;
- retaining the provider payload itself;
- preserving historical facts about what was observed;
- publishing/redistributing content.

Those may have different legal, contractual, evidentiary, and publication lifecycles.

This unresolved question is the basis for current deferred item F16. No exception to immutable
Vault doctrine has been authorized.

Material external sources cited during reconciliation included:

- X Developer Policy: https://docs.x.com/developer-terms/policy
- X Developer Agreement: https://docs.x.com/developer-terms/agreement
- X Terms of Service: https://x.com/en/tos

No legal conclusion is preserved here.

## Social acquisition direction preserved

The report surveyed the official X API, browser/manual capture, Zeeschuimer, 4CAT, account
scrapers, archive exports, and media downloaders.

Useful conceptual findings included:

- provider payloads should not dictate Desk Record shape;
- thread/conversation relationships may need to be derived rather than trusted;
- acquisition route and session class matter to provenance;
- relays are acquisition origins/routes, not proof of original source identity;
- exact JSON-field addressing could eventually motivate a JSON Locator if a real File needs
  it.

None of those implementation ideas were promoted. X/social remains deferred until a real File
or approved outward operation requires it and retention/policy questions are resolved first.

## Living report and operator-use finding

Fable's most important non-provider concern was operator usefulness.

It argued that a technically sound Record can still fail as a Product if:

- admitting one useful Observation requires too much command/UUID plumbing;
- investigators have no sanctioned loose Workspace;
- the living report hides source limits, excerpts, custody/retrieval limitations, or history
  already present in the Record;
- models cannot read frozen Surfaces through a useful governed seam.

The report recommended measuring these pressures during real DD-7225 commissioning rather
than pre-building a UI or workflow platform.

## Recommendations preserved

The report's high-level recommendations, after Steward reconciliation, reduce to:

### Do through the current milestone

- commission real FILE-01;
- measure operator/research friction;
- keep ordinary working material visibly outside Record;
- ensure irreplaceable real work has an intentional preservation/restore posture;
- let the real File expose what report, admission, Surface/Locator, and model-assistance
  improvements are actually earned.

### Research/revisit when triggered

- model-assisted exact evidence noticing against frozen Surfaces;
- richer living-report projection;
- batch/reviewable admission only if measured friction warrants it;
- whole-media or whole-document derived Surfaces only if citation volume warrants it;
- provider-restricted/deletable evidence lifecycle before any affected provider path;
- source genealogy when manual retelling analysis becomes repetitive.

### Keep deliberately deferred

- broad OCR/media platforms;
- search/graph/semantic retrieval;
- entity-resolution machinery;
- autonomous research/monitoring;
- X/social integration;
- public website and Quinton production until an outward artifact is selected;
- multi-user/remote production access.

### Reject as Product direction

- a single monolithic “investigative engine”;
- arbitrary model SQL/database authority;
- governed confidence/risk/suspicion/truth scores;
- provider-native payload schemas as Desk Record architecture;
- fuzzy re-anchoring that silently rewrites evidence Locators;
- unrestricted semantic JSONB escape hatches.

## Steward reconciliation notes

The following report statements were specifically narrowed during Steward review:

1. “Nothing else has a Locator” was too strong. External systems have selector/anchor/span
   concepts. The more defensible differentiation is the Desk's combined frozen-target,
   Locator, Excerpt, Observation/Claim/Decision, append-only-history, and mechanical-walkback
   discipline.
2. Anthropic Citations is promising, but no provider is architectural authority and no claim
   that Anthropic is uniquely capable should become doctrine without a current provider
   comparison.
3. Model proposal provenance is a real future pressure, but the report's “one schema line”
   framing was premature. The correct durable direction is to preserve meaningful model
   participation without deciding today whether that belongs on admission, proposal,
   Workspace, or future Run lineage.
4. X retention-status/tombstone architecture was not accepted. The policy question must be
   resolved before any affected acquisition design.
5. Backup/restore is a strong operational recommendation, but was not silently promoted from
   historical design material into a current FILE-01 authority gate.
