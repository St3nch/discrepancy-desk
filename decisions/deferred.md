# Discrepancy Desk Deferred Register

Deferred means deliberately unbuilt. An entry preserves intent, trigger, and cost of
forgetting; it does not smuggle a design or ticket into the backlog.

This file is also the canonical Desk future-direction register. Material future ideas may
come from CHAZ, the Steward, Writers, reviewers, LLM research, external research, operator
friction, incidents, or real File work. Origin does not grant authority.

Durable entries use one of two dispositions:

- **Accepted Direction** — CHAZ and the Steward agree the capability belongs in the
  Product if/when its conditions are met. It is not backlog or implementation authority.
- **Research Required** — the question matters but the project does not yet have enough
  evidence to accept a direction.

Promotion is always a fresh Product decision. A trigger firing never creates a ticket by
itself.

## Four-Horizon Clock

- **CURRENT** — deliberately revisit during the active milestone.
- **NEXT** — revisit at the next named milestone or post-commissioning review.
- **TRIGGERED** — revisit when a named observable condition occurs.
- **HORIZON** — long-range; revisit during major Product review or re-onboarding.

The Clock says when to reconsider an item, not when to build it.

## Discrepancy Desk Product Pillars

Every durable future item names one primary Pillar:

1. **Evidence & Sources** — acquisition, custody, Artifact/Surface/Locator integrity,
   source reuse, and evidence-preservation boundaries.
2. **Investigation & Reasoning** — research, noticing, source comparison, Claims,
   Discrepancies, retrieval, and bounded model assistance.
3. **Operator & Governance** — Workspace, operator ergonomics, identifiers, access,
   authority, reliability, and governed working method.
4. **Rendition & Publication** — living/public projections, Quinton, publication history,
   platform presentation, and outward-facing artifacts.

The Pillar says where the idea belongs in the Product. Provider, model, library, database,
and ticket names are not Pillars.

Each durable entry preserves its disposition, Pillar, Clock, direction/question, why it
matters, why not now, promotion trigger, deliberate revisit point, evidence basis, cost of
forgetting, and explicit `Not authorized` boundary. Do not pre-design the eventual
implementation inside this register.

## F1 — Research source and method library

**Disposition:** Accepted Direction

**Pillar:** Evidence & Sources

**Clock:** NEXT

**Direction:** The Desk should accumulate useful websites, archives, catalogs, books,
investigative methods, access constraints, and source-specific lessons so later Files do
not rediscover the same research terrain.

**Why it matters:** Reusable source and method knowledge should survive individual research
sessions without turning weak mirrors or secondary summaries into authority.

**Why not now:** FILE-01 must first expose what information is actually reusable:
institutional source identity, acquisition routes, search tactics, access limitations,
credibility or custody cautions, and topic-specific versus general research value.

**Promotion trigger:** The Rendlesham investigation produces repeated source-discovery
knowledge that would materially improve another research pass or File.

**Revisit point:** After real `DD-7225` runtime commissioning and its first research/friction
review.

**Evidence basis:** VISION research doctrine plus FILE-01 corpus-verification lessons about
mirrors, custody, acquisition routes, and HTTP-success limits.

**Cost of forgetting:** Research becomes chat memory and repeated browsing, while weak
mirrors or secondary summaries are mistaken for source authority.

**Not authorized:** No source-library schema, crawler, UI, provider adapter, or generalized
research platform.

## F2 — Broad Workspace and notebook experience

**Disposition:** Accepted Direction

**Pillar:** Operator & Governance

**Clock:** NEXT

**Direction:** The Desk will eventually need comfortable support for notes, leads,
questions, todos, snippets, hypotheses, and story ideas.

**Why it matters:** Investigation needs a sanctioned loose space so exploratory work does not
become shadow Record or disappear across sessions.

**Why not now:** FILE-01 needs only enough operator workflow to complete one real File.
Workspace must not become Record by convenience.

**Promotion trigger:** Real investigation is materially slowed by handling ordinary working
material outside the Product.

**Revisit point:** After real `DD-7225` commissioning, using measured operator friction rather
than anticipated UI preference.

**Evidence basis:** D3 Workspace/Record separation and the active FILE-01 operator workflow.

**Cost of forgetting:** Researchers create scattered shadow records or pressure the Record
model to absorb ungoverned notes.

**Not authorized:** No notebook application, Workspace database, broad local UI, or automatic
promotion of notes into Record.

## F3 — General OCR and document-processing platform

detection, and improved inspection Surfaces.

**Why it matters:** Repeated document processing should remain reproducible and lineage-bound

**Not authorized:** No general OCR service, layout platform, extractor fleet, automatic
Observation admission, or replacement of captured Artifacts with derived text.

## F5 — Source genealogy and narrative-mutation analysis

**Disposition:** Accepted Direction

**Pillar:** Investigation & Reasoning

**Clock:** NEXT

**Direction:** The Desk should identify reprints, near-clones, quote inheritance, common
origin, qualifier loss, wording changes, added actors or motives, and precision that
appears without new evidence.

**Why it matters:** Source count must not masquerade as independence, and later retellings
must not quietly overwrite contemporaneous testimony.

**Why not now:** One File can preserve explicit source dependence manually. Broad reusable
machinery needs repeated examples.

**Promotion trigger:** Multiple sources or Files make independence and retelling lineage a
recurring investigative question.

**Revisit point:** After the first real `DD-7225` living report explicitly states what its
accepted corpus can and cannot establish about later retellings.

**Evidence basis:** VISION's contemporaneous-vs-retelling doctrine and DD-7225's working
investigation question.

**Cost of forgetting:** Source volume is mistaken for independent corroboration and later
retellings quietly overwrite earlier testimony.

**Not authorized:** No genealogy engine, global source graph, automated independence score,
or corpus expansion without separate reconciliation.

## F6 — General Entity identity resolution

**Disposition:** Accepted Direction

**Pillar:** Investigation & Reasoning

**Clock:** TRIGGERED

**Direction:** Identity resolution must eventually support reversible merge, split, and
conflict history without rewriting evidence provenance.

**Why it matters:** A File must be able to distinguish or connect real people, organizations,
places, and aliases without making names accidental identity.

**Why not now:** FILE-01 does not require a general Entity system unless its real corpus
proves otherwise.

**Promotion trigger:** A File cannot honestly connect or distinguish people, organizations,
places, or aliases without durable governed resolution.

**Revisit point:** At FILE-02 closure if no earlier File triggers it.

**Evidence basis:** Current identity/provenance doctrine plus the absence of demonstrated
FILE-01 pressure for a general Entity system.

**Cost of forgetting:** Names become accidental identity, or later correction requires
destructive rewriting.

**Not authorized:** No universal ontology, entity graph, merge engine, or identity resolver
before a real File cannot proceed honestly without one.

## F7 — Broad search, graph, and semantic retrieval

**Disposition:** Accepted Direction

**Pillar:** Investigation & Reasoning

**Clock:** TRIGGERED

**Direction:** Full-text search, embeddings, graph projections, and bounded semantic
retrieval may improve discovery over accumulated Record.

**Why it matters:** Access aids may eventually make accumulated Record usable without becoming
a second authority layer.

**Why not now:** They are rebuildable access aids, not Record authority, and one File does
not justify a platform.

**Promotion trigger:** Measured investigation or evidence-walkback work becomes materially
difficult with direct governed reads.

**Revisit point:** After the second completed real File if measured pain has not triggered
review earlier.

**Evidence basis:** D6/D10 rebuildable-state doctrine and the current one-File scale.

**Cost of forgetting:** Operators may build ungoverned shadow indexes or confuse a
similarity result with evidence.

**Not authorized:** No search platform, embeddings store, graph database, semantic index,
or durable Record dependency on rebuildable retrieval state.

## F8 — Autonomous research and scheduled monitoring

**Disposition:** Accepted Direction

**Pillar:** Investigation & Reasoning

**Clock:** TRIGGERED

**Direction:** Models may eventually help discover candidates, monitor sources, and
perform bounded recurring research.

**Why it matters:** Repeated bounded research may eventually benefit from automation while
human scope, capture, spend, and admission authority remain explicit.

**Why not now:** Authority, scope, spend, capture, source safety, and admission behavior
must first be proven through human-directed real work.

**Promotion trigger:** Repeated Files establish a bounded research operation whose inputs,
outputs, refusals, and human checkpoints can be stated precisely.

**Revisit point:** During the first major Product review after multiple completed real Files.

**Evidence basis:** AGENTS model-authority boundaries and the requirement to prove
human-directed work before recurring automation.

**Cost of forgetting:** Valuable sources are missed; premature implementation instead
creates an unsupervised content harvester.

**Not authorized:** No autonomous agent, scheduled acquisition, provider spend, automatic
Capture, or model-to-Record admission path.

## F13 — Multi-user, remote, and production access

**Disposition:** Accepted Direction

**Pillar:** Operator & Governance

**Clock:** TRIGGERED

**Direction:** Authentication, authorization, multi-user editing, remote service exposure,
and hosted operations may eventually be required.

**Why it matters:** A local single-operator trust boundary must not be mistaken for a safe
remote or multi-user production boundary.

**Why not now:** The Desk is a local single-operator Product and FILE-01 does not require a
network service. This defers production human authentication and multi-user access; it does
not waive FILE-01's positive capability separation between ordinary/model-facing Record work
and human-authorized Decision admission.

**Promotion trigger:** A second human operator or non-local production client needs governed
access.

**Revisit point:** Before granting a second operator or non-local client any Desk access.

**Evidence basis:** The local single-operator Product boundary plus FILE-01's deliberately
bounded capability separation.

**Cost of forgetting:** A local trust boundary could be exposed as though it were
production-safe.

**Not authorized:** No auth service, remote exposure, hosted multi-user workflow,
collaboration layer, or production access model.

## F14 — Additional acquisition providers and automation

**Disposition:** Accepted Direction

**Pillar:** Evidence & Sources

**Clock:** TRIGGERED

**Direction:** Archives, official APIs, web capture tools, and specialist providers may
eventually support repeatable acquisition.

**Why it matters:** Some future sources may require acquisition routes beyond manual local
files, but provider machinery should exist only when real evidence cannot be captured
faithfully or safely otherwise.

**Why not now:** FILE-01 uses a deliberately small manually verified corpus. The existence
of a tool or provider does not create a Product requirement.

**Promotion trigger:** A concrete source cannot be captured faithfully through the accepted
path, or repeated manual acquisition creates demonstrated cost or risk.

**Revisit point:** At FILE-02 acquisition planning if no earlier source triggers it.

**Evidence basis:** FILE-01's successful bounded manual acquisition path and its observed
custody/integrity limitations.

**Cost of forgetting:** Valuable evidence may remain inaccessible; premature adapters
would create infrastructure without investigative demand.

**Not authorized:** No provider framework, scheduled ingestion, browser automation, archive
adapter, credential flow, or generalized acquisition service.

## F15 — Model-assisted exact evidence noticing against frozen Surfaces

**Disposition:** Accepted Direction

**Pillar:** Investigation & Reasoning

**Clock:** NEXT

**Direction:** Models may help read frozen Desk Surfaces and propose materially relevant
passages, candidate Observations, Claims, or Discrepancy questions when their evidence
locations can be mechanically rechecked against Desk-controlled frozen material. Provider
features such as Anthropic Citations are optional implementation experiments, not Product
architecture.

**Why it matters:** Model-assisted reading could materially reduce manual evidence-location
work while preserving the Desk rule that models notice, governed Record preserves, and the
human decides.

**Why not now:** FILE-01 has not yet measured real operator reading/citation friction, and no
model proposal/admission workflow has been proven against real Record material.

**Promotion trigger:** Real File work shows that manually locating and binding evidence
materially limits investigation or model-assisted review.

**Revisit point:** After real `DD-7225` commissioning and its friction review.

**Evidence basis:** September 2026 Fable ecosystem research, Steward reconciliation, and
CHAZ acceptance of provider-independent model-assisted exact evidence locating as a future
direction.

**Cost of forgetting:** Later model integration may default to floating citations, raw-PDF
chat, or provider-specific references that cannot become durable Desk Locators.

**Not authorized:** No Anthropic dependency, provider selection, API integration, model
database role, proposal table, new actor schema, automatic Observation/Claim admission, or
implementation ticket.

## F16 — Retention lifecycle for provider-restricted or deletable evidence

**Disposition:** Research Required

**Pillar:** Evidence & Sources

**Clock:** TRIGGERED

**Question:** How can the Desk honestly represent historically important material when the
route used to obtain it carries deletion, modification, redistribution, or retention
obligations that may conflict with immutable Vault preservation?

**Why it matters:** Evidence availability, historical assertion, internal citation,
publication, and redistribution can have different rights and lifecycles. Treating access
as permission to preserve forever could create contractual/legal risk; silently erasing
history could break Desk walkback doctrine.

**Why not now:** No active File requires provider-restricted material, and the answer may
depend on the exact provider, acquisition route, agreement, and publication use.

**Promotion trigger:** A real File requires material whose acquisition or retention terms
conflict with ordinary immutable preservation, or a provider integration with deletion or
synchronization obligations is proposed.

**Revisit point:** Before authorizing the first such acquisition/provider path; also during
any future F11 social-acquisition review.

**Evidence basis:** September 2026 X/API retention-policy research plus CHAZ/Steward
discussion distinguishing source payload, historical reporting, and later source deletion.

**Cost of forgetting:** The Desk could either retain material it was required to remove or
destroy evidence history without an explicit governed doctrine for why walkback changed.

**Not authorized:** No X connector, tombstone/retention-status schema, provider-policy
workaround, payload deletion mechanism, legal conclusion, or exception to immutable Vault
doctrine.
